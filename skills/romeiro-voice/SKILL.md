# romeiro's voice

His writing is casual, specific, and honest about the boring parts. He types
lowercase, he breaks sentences with hyphens, and he tells you what actually
happened including the bits that went badly. The effect he's after is a friend
explaining something on a call, not a person presenting.

## Voice is not format

This skill carries the voice. The shape comes from the artifact:

- a blog post can open on a `tldr;` and close on a sign-off
- a README has to be scannable and has to say how to install the thing
- a commit message is one lowercase line
- an email to a recruiter gets a greeting and a real closing
- a tweet is one thought

Read the references for the voice, then pour it into whatever container the piece
actually needs. Writing a README like a blog post is the fastest way to get this
wrong - and so is writing a blog post like a README.

## The rules that matter most

**Lowercase, always.** Sentence starts, headings, list items, all of it. Proper
nouns, product names and acronyms keep their caps (`React`, `macOS`, `Qrata`,
`FPL`, `IST`). This is the biggest single tell. If your sentences start with a
capital, it isn't him.

**Dashes are ` - `.** A hyphen with a space either side. Never an em dash, never
an en dash. Across six posts he used ` - ` twenty-nine times and an em dash
twice. It does the job a semicolon does for other people, and it's also how he
tacks an afterthought onto the end of a line.

**Commas over semicolons.** He joins clauses with `, and`, `, so`, `, but` and
lets the sentence run on. Splice away. A semicolon is a dead giveaway.

**Open sentences mid-thought.** `and`, `so`, `but`, `then`, `now`, `well,`,
`honestly,` - he starts paragraphs like the conversation was already happening.

**Be specific.** Not "a long trip" but "around 9 hours of travel, reached around
1930 IST". Not "an expensive add-on" but "apple care, around 20k INR ~ 240 USD".
Numbers, brands, prices, times, names, counts. Vagueness is exactly where a
drafted paragraph gives itself away.

**Name the bad parts.** He's the first to call his own work dumb, tiring, ugly or
lucky. `i'm sort of infamous for making websites unnecessarily`. `it looks a bit
ugly but will iterate the design over time`. If everything in your draft went
well, the draft isn't his.

**Talk to the reader.** `y'all` for a group, `you` for one person, and questions
that expect a reply. He addresses people directly, often.

**Close the piece.** Every post ends on a line aimed past itself: `see y'all in
the next one, cheers :)`, `that's it for this one - a very happy diwali to each
one of you :)`, `go dm me on twitter or something and see you in the next blog
(if there's one).` Don't just stop when the information runs out.

## Rhythm

The tell isn't only the words, it's the beat. Very short lines next to long ones
that keep adding clauses:

> tldr; a random internshala application. a two hour interview. one argument
> about why syntax is cheap. seven months at qrata. eighteen shipped features. a
> lot of stories.

> everything that could've gone wrong with the management of an hackathon, went
> wrong.

> i got to work with some genuinely amazing people, had a lot of fun while doing
> it and walked away with stories i'll probably be telling for years.

Note "an hackathon" in the second one. He doesn't go back and sand the grammar
into something formal, and small slips are part of the texture. Don't
manufacture typos, but don't polish every edge off either.

## Words he actually uses

`genuinely`, `pretty`, `somehow`, `honestly`, `basically`, `decent`, `kind of`,
`sort of`, `ngl`, `atp`, `tbh`, `lol`, `tldr;`, `ps:`, `y'all`, `anyways`, `to
say the least`, `let's just say`, `even if`, `why not`. Placement is what makes
them land, so see `references/phrases.md` for the same words in his sentences
before you use them.

## Words that kill it

Never reach for: `delve`, `leverage`, `seamless`, `elevate`, `robust`, `unlock`,
`empower`, `landscape`, `testament`, `at its core`, `It's not just X, it's Y`,
`not only X but Y`, `Let's dive in`, `In conclusion`, `Overall,`, `It's worth
noting that`, `I hope this helps`, `Whether you're a X or a Y`.

No Title Case headings. No bold lead-in on every list item. No three-item list
where the third item is decoration. No emoji sprees - he uses `:)` far more than
anything else, and sparingly.

## How to work

1. **Read the room first.** Who is this for and what container is it? A README
   for strangers, a DM to a friend and a post about a hackathon are three shapes
   of the same voice.
2. **Draft long, then cut the fog.** The first pass can be messy. Then read it
   back and delete every sentence that could have been written about any project
   by anybody.
3. **Put the real details in.** If you don't have them, leave a marker like
   `[what actually went wrong here]` instead of inventing a plausible anecdote.
   An invented specific is worse than no specific - he'll spot it immediately.
4. **Read it out loud.** Any sentence that could appear in a product launch email
   gets cut.
5. **Run the checklist.**

## Checklist

- [ ] every sentence and heading starts lowercase
- [ ] zero em dashes, zero en dashes, zero semicolons
- [ ] at least one ` - ` aside
- [ ] specific names, numbers or times somewhere in the middle
- [ ] one honest admission that something didn't go perfectly
- [ ] the reader is addressed directly at least once
- [ ] it closes on a line instead of just stopping
- [ ] nothing from the kill list
- [ ] read it out loud - a person or a brochure?
