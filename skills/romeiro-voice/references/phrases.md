# the phrase bank

Every line below is his, taken from his own posts. Placement is the point - the
same words in a different spot read like a costume. Use these as shapes to think
in, not as a list to sprinkle.

## opening a piece

He almost never opens with a thesis. He opens like he's picking up a
conversation that already started.

> well, i thought of doing something like this for quite a while now but finally
> got some time today so here we are.

> well this blog hasn't been sacked off yet. it has been around a month since the
> last blog. the main reason was the mid sem exams.

> call this blog lazarus because it has risen from the dead. a whole month later,
> here i am with hackathon lore - it's the only action in my life atp :(

The move: admit the gap, joke about it, then get going. `so here we are`, `a whole
month later`, `it has been around a month since the last one`.

## the tldr line

Long pieces open with one. It's the entire thing compressed into a single long
line, allowed to run on, usually ending in an aside or a shrug.

> tldr; i'm romeiro, i build websites. i needed a way to share things i build,
> found interesting, and learned. having a personal blog didn't seem that bad of
> a decision to me so here we are :)

> tldr; a random internshala application. a two hour interview. one argument
> about why syntax is cheap. seven months at qrata. eighteen shipped features. a
> lot of stories.

> tldr; got the new macbook m4, mid sem exams done with decent marks, didn't
> qualify internal round of sih and my internship's ending in a week. added the
> hackathons page too at blog.romeiro.dev/hackathons

Note the semicolon after `tldr` and the fragment form in the second one. That
staccato version - noun phrase, full stop, noun phrase - is worth stealing when
the piece has a sequence to it.

## moving the story along

Short posts live and die on this. He restarts sentences constantly.

`and then`, `so`, `but then`, `now`, `then`, `eventually`, `anyways`,
`honestly,`, `turns out`, `funny how`, `the main reason was`, `what else -`,
`at this point`.

> honestly, i was wondering how i got approached by a company in the first place.
> turns out it was one of my applications via internshala that i'd completely
> forgotten about.

> funny how an application i didn't even remember applying for ended up giving me
> seven months i'll never forget.

`turns out` and `funny how` are his tells for the small reversal in a story. Use
one when something doesn't go the obvious way.

## intensifiers and hedges

The ones he reaches for, roughly in order of how often they show up:

`genuinely`, `pretty`, `somehow`, `honestly`, `basically`, `decent`, `kind of`,
`sort of`, `at least`, `i hope`, `idk`, `arguably`, `to say the least`.

> there were so many things that lined up perfectly for us winning this thing
> that it's genuinely mind boggling.

> the problem statements of bnb 2025 were arguably the hardest and weirdest ones
> i've seen in a hackathon so far.

> only one problem statement was readable and doable from the web and aiml domain

`genuinely` and `pretty` carry most of the enthusiasm, so they stay convincing
where `absolutely incredible` would sound like marketing. He almost never writes
`very` twice in a paragraph.

## asides

Two containers, and he uses both constantly.

**Parentheses** for a fact, an aside, or a translation of what he just said:

> we reached there at around 1930 IST (after around 9 hours of travel) and then
> were registered in and given rooms and zones.

> i got the new macbook m4 base variant (16gb ram, 256 gb ssd) with apple care
> plus (3 years of warranty for physical and liquid damages)

**A spaced hyphen** for the afterthought that lands after the sentence is
technically over:

> tldr; turned 20, qualified for bnb 25 finals, lost bnb 25 but got 2nd in
> college - (the 1st place team from college got 3rd place overall), on a hunt for
> the next internship and exploring web3.

> that's it for this one - a very happy diwali to each one of you :)

The pattern is: say the thing plainly, then add the detail that makes it real.

## introducing a list

He writes the lead-in as a sentence that ends on a hyphen, then puts the items on
their own lines.

> i wasn't interested in this hackathon because of three reasons -
>
> the number of participants (a lot more got filtered out too to make into the
> top 600 teams)
>
> location (it was in another city, longer travel than usual)
>
> 36 hours (3 days tbh)

> the main differences that i have felt while using the mac are -
>
> godly display
> unreal battery backup
> dia browser

Items are lowercase, no full stops, no bold, and often carry a parenthetical.
`godly display` next to `unreal battery backup` next to a browser name is exactly
the register - it's a note to a friend, not a spec sheet.

## being honest about the bad parts

This is the part that's hardest to fake and most worth getting right.

> i'm sort of infamous for making websites unnecessarily.

> it looks a bit ugly but will iterate the design over time.

> i did not expect to get a win in this hackathon.

> now the 3 people who im going to forward this link are going to have a laugh
> because they think it was a dumb decision to buy the apple care (around 20k INR
> ~ 240 USD), i hope its not.

> at a 35% win rate now lol.

The shape: state the unflattering version first, in the plainest words available,
then move on without defending it. Never sandwich it in a compliments. `it looks
a bit ugly` is the whole review.

## talking to the reader

`y'all` when it's a group, `you` when it's one person, and he'll ask questions he
isn't expecting an answer to.

> that's it for this blog, looks long enough. see the 4 of y'all in the next one <3

> to everyone at qrata, thank you for making my first proper software engineering
> internship one i'll always remember.

> the 3 people who im going to forward this link

He'll also count his actual readers, which is funnier than saying `all of you`.

## closing

Every post ends looking forward, and it's usually two parts: a short verdict on
the piece itself, then the sign-off.

> anyways, see y'all in the next blog (yeah there's gonna be more of these, i
> hope).

> that's it for this one - a very happy diwali to each one of you :)

> see you in the next one where i'll probably discuss how i got my new internship
> after this hackathon.

> go dm me on twitter or something and see you in the next blog (if there's one).

> see y'all in the next one, cheers :)

`see y'all in the next one` is the default. `that's it for this one` is the other
one. Both beat stopping.

## reactions

`lol` for something mildly absurd or self-inflicted. `wooohooo!!` exactly once,
for a win. `:(` for a loss. `<3` for people. `XD` and `:p` when something is
genuinely funny. `:)` for almost everything else, which is the important part -
it's the default tone marker, and it appears far more than any other.

> ps: got my sem 4 sgpa while writing (9.46) - highest out of all the sems so
> far, wooohooo!!

`ps:` is how he adds one more thing after the piece has already closed.

## small habits worth copying

- `im`, `ive`, `id`, `youre` without the apostrophe turn up in his posts. Don't
  sprinkle these in on purpose, but don't be precious about them either.
- he names the software: `dia browser`, `n8n`, `shiki`, `arch linux`, `Lenovo
  S340`, `instrument serif`. A generic noun where a name would do reads dead.
- prices come in both currencies: `20k INR ~ 240 USD`.
- times come in 24-hour with a zone: `1930 IST`.
- he writes `an hackathon`. Occasionally the article doesn't match. It stays in.
